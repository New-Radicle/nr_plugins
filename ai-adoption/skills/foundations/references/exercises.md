# Twenty-minute exercises by layer

One exercise per layer for each broad kind of work. Pick the row that matches the person, do the exercise on their real material, and note what surprised them. Nothing here needs any connector.

## Writing-heavy roles (proposals, reports, communications, grants, funder updates)

| Layer | Exercise | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask for the same 200-word summary of a document twice. Compare. | Where the two differ tells you what to verify. |
| 2 | Paste two past documents you are proud of, then ask for a new one "in the same voice and structure". | How much closer it gets with examples than without. |
| 3 | Find out which of your systems your assistant can already reach. Ask it to read something from one of them. | What it could do without you pasting. |
| 4 | Ask it to draft an email but not send it. Check where it would have stopped. | Where the pause is, and whether you want it earlier. |
| 5 | Ask for three sources on a claim in your field, then check each one exists. | How confident the wrong ones sounded. |

## Technical roles (engineering, data, lab, field)

| Layer | Exercise | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Give it a calculation with a known answer, then a harder one. Ask it to show its steps. | Where reasoning holds and where it slips. |
| 2 | Paste a spec or protocol and ask it to write the test report skeleton in your format. | How much of your format it needs to be told. |
| 3 | Ask it to read a data file and summarise it, instead of pasting the numbers. | Tool use versus copy-paste. |
| 4 | Ask it to run a multi-step task on a copy of some data, with a stop before any write. | What it decided alone and what it asked. |
| 5 | Have it draft a technical claim, then verify against the primary source. Log any error. | The review process is the product. |

## Operations, finance, people, compliance

| Layer | Exercise | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask what today's date is and what it knows about your company. | Its cutoff and its blindness to you. |
| 2 | Feed it your policy or checklist and ask it to apply it to a case. | Rules it applied correctly, rules it missed. |
| 3 | Ask what it can reach in your calendar or task system, then have it list, not create. | Read before write. |
| 4 | Describe a weekly task and ask what would need to be true to run it on a schedule safely. | Permissions, records, failure handling. |
| 5 | Ask it to redact personal data from a sample document, then check every field. | What redaction misses. |

## Leaders and sponsors

| Layer | Exercise | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask it to explain a decision you made last quarter as if to a new hire. | What it gets wrong without context. |
| 2 | Now paste the board memo and ask again. | The difference is layer 2. |
| 3 | Ask which of your team's systems it could work inside, and what it would need. | Where the integration wall is. |
| 4 | Ask for a weekly digest of one thing you track, and where it should stop and ask you. | What you would let run alone. |
| 5 | Ask it to produce a number you can check. Check it. Say what you found in front of the team. | You just modelled the first-error response. |
