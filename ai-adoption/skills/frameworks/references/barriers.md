# Five psychological barriers

Individual objections, each with the intervention that works. Use these in coaching, workshop design, and handbook language.

| Barrier | How people say it | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Perceived opacity | "I can't see how it thinks." "I don't trust the output." | Show the reasoning. Ask for the comparison, the sources, the alternative it rejected. |
| Perceived lack of nuance | "It doesn't get our context." "It can't handle judgment calls." | Frame tasks objectively. Position AI as the data handler and drafter, the person as the judge. |
| Perceived inflexibility | "It gives the same generic thing every time." | Show iteration. Multi-turn refinement, custom instructions, examples of their own past work as style guides. |
| Autonomy threat | "If it does my work, what's left for me?" | Human-in-the-loop by design. AI drafts, people decide. Smart defaults they can override. |
| Preference for people | "I'd rather just do it myself or ask a colleague." | Replace drudgework, not human interaction. The time saved goes to people, not to more screens. |

## Mapping objections to personas

Skeptics most often voice opacity and nuance. Traditionalists voice inflexibility and preference for people. Conformists rarely voice anything; they wait. Autonomy threat appears in any persona whose role is being reshaped, and it needs the Sponsor's answer, not the Rollout Lead's.
